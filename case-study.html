SECTION 01
Business Problem

An airline company operates across 15 domestic Indian routes with fluctuating ticket demand across seasons, booking windows, and travel classes. Management identified that revenue per available seat (RASK) was inconsistent — some routes were underpriced during peak demand, while others saw excess empty seats at off-peak times.

The core business question: "What variables most significantly influence ticket pricing, and how can we restructure pricing tiers to maximize revenue per route?"

KEY QUESTIONS THIS ANALYSIS ANSWERS
Q1Which routes generate the highest revenue, and what makes them profitable?
Q2How does booking window (days before travel) affect ticket pricing and seat availability?
Q3What is the revenue difference between economy and business class, and is there an upsell opportunity?
Q4Which seasons drive demand spikes, and how should fares respond?

SECTION 03
Data Cleaning Steps
The raw dataset required significant preparation before analysis. Below are the specific cleaning operations performed in Python using Pandas.
01
Removed 340 duplicate rows — identified using df.duplicated(). These were exact duplicates across all columns likely from scraping the same listing multiple times.
02
Handled 12 null ticket prices — rows with missing price values were dropped since price is the target variable and cannot be imputed without introducing bias.
03
Converted date_of_journey from string to datetime — raw format was "09/06/2019", converted using pd.to_datetime(df['date_of_journey'], dayfirst=True).
04
Converted duration to integer minutes — raw format was "2h 50m". Split on 'h' and 'm', then computed hours × 60 + minutes.
05
Engineered season feature from travel month: Summer (Apr–Jun), Monsoon (Jul–Sep), Winter (Oct–Dec), Off-peak (Jan–Mar). Seasonal demand patterns are a key pricing driver.
06
Created booking_bucket feature — binned days_before_travel into 4 groups: 0–7 (Last Minute), 8–14, 15–30 (Optimal), 30+ (Early Bird). Enables segmentation analysis.
07
Removed price outliers — 47 rows with prices beyond 3 standard deviations (above ₹42,000 or below ₹800) were flagged as likely data errors and removed. Final dataset: 12,241 clean rows.
PYTHON CODE SNIPPET — DATA CLEANING

KEY EDA FINDINGS
DISTRIBUTION FINDING
Ticket prices are right-skewed — median (₹4,312) is significantly lower than mean (₹5,841), pulled upward by high-value business class and last-minute bookings. A log transformation was applied before regression modeling.
CORRELATION FINDING
Strong negative correlation (r = −0.62) between days_before_travel and price — the earlier the booking, the lower the price. This was the strongest single predictor in the dataset.
ROUTE FINDING
Delhi→Mumbai and Delhi→Bangalore together account for 33.8% of all bookings and 41% of total revenue — high concentration risk but also the best dynamic pricing opportunity.

STATISTICAL FINDINGS SUMMARY
r = −0.62
Booking window vs. price: The strongest correlation found — earlier bookings are significantly cheaper. Pearson correlation of −0.62 (p < 0.001) confirms this as the dominant pricing signal in the data.
2.4×
Business class revenue yield: Business class tickets generate 2.4× the revenue per seat compared to economy. Despite representing only 18.2% of bookings, they contribute 31.4% of total revenue — a clear upsell priority.
+38%
Seasonal price premium: Summer season fares are 38% higher than off-peak on average. A one-way ANOVA confirmed statistically significant price differences across all four seasons (F = 142.3, p < 0.001).
−16%
Stops impact on price: Each additional stop reduces ticket price by approximately 16%, controlling for route and class. This reflects consumer preference for direct flights, which airlines can leverage as a premium pricing signal.
R² = 0.71
Model fit: The regression model explains 71% of the variance in log(price). The remaining 29% is attributable to factors not in the dataset — such as promotional sales, competitor pricing, or real-time demand.
SECTION 06
SQL Queries Used in Analysis
Data extraction and aggregation were performed in SQL before Python analysis. Below are the key queries that generated the metrics used throughout this report.
QUERY 1 — REVENUE BY ROUTE WITH MARKET SHARE
-- Revenue concentration analysis SELECT route, COUNT(*) AS bookings, SUM(price) AS total_revenue, ROUND(SUM(price) * 100.0 / SUM(SUM(price)) OVER(), 1) AS revenue_share_pct FROM flights GROUP BY route ORDER BY total_revenue DESC; -- Result: DEL→BOM + DEL→BLR = 33.8% of bookings, 41% of revenue
QUERY 2 — BOOKING WINDOW PRICE ANALYSIS
-- Price by booking window bucket SELECT CASE WHEN days_before_travel BETWEEN 0 AND 7 THEN 'Last Minute' WHEN days_before_travel BETWEEN 8 AND 14 THEN '8-14 Days' WHEN days_before_travel BETWEEN 15 AND 30 THEN '15-30 Days' ELSE 'Early Bird' END AS booking_window, ROUND(AVG(price), 0) AS avg_price, ROUND(AVG(seat_availability), 1) AS avg_availability_pct FROM flights GROUP BY booking_window ORDER BY avg_price DESC; -- Result: 15-30 days = ₹4,280 avg price, 91% seat availability (sweet spot)
QUERY 3 — CLASS REVENUE CONTRIBUTION (CTE)
-- Business vs Economy revenue share WITH class_rev AS ( SELECT travel_class, COUNT(*) AS bookings, SUM(price) AS revenue FROM flights GROUP BY travel_class ) SELECT travel_class, bookings, ROUND(bookings * 100.0 / SUM(bookings) OVER(), 1) AS booking_share_pct, ROUND(revenue * 100.0 / SUM(revenue) OVER(), 1) AS revenue_share_pct FROM class_rev; -- Business: 18.2% of bookings → 31.4% of revenue (2.4× yield multiplier)

SECTION 08
Business Recommendations
Based on the statistical findings and EDA, the following recommendations are structured by priority and expected revenue impact.
● REC 01 — HIGH PRIORITY · DYNAMIC PRICING ON TOP 3 ROUTES
Finding: DEL→BOM and DEL→BLR account for 41% of revenue and show the strongest booking-window price elasticity (r = −0.71 on these routes specifically).

Action: Implement a 3-tier dynamic pricing model on these routes: raise fares by 30–35% for bookings within 7 days, hold current rates for 8–30 day bookings, and offer 10% Early Bird discounts for 31+ day bookings to increase early revenue visibility and load factor certainty.
● REC 02 — HIGH PRIORITY · BUSINESS CLASS UPSELL CAMPAIGN
Finding: Business class generates 2.4× revenue per seat but only represents 18.2% of bookings. The gap suggests underpromotion rather than lack of demand, particularly on the Delhi–Mumbai corridor.

Action: Launch a targeted upsell campaign at the 48–72 hour pre-departure window when economy passengers have committed to travel. Offer business class upgrades at 40–50% of the full business class differential. Even a 2% conversion rate at this margin would increase route RASK by an estimated 4.8%.
● REC 03 — MEDIUM PRIORITY · OFF-PEAK DEMAND STIMULATION
Finding: Off-peak (Jan–Mar) routes show average 34% lower seat occupancy (inferred from availability data) and average prices only 12% lower than peak — meaning fares are not discounted enough to stimulate discretionary travel.

Action: Reduce off-peak economy fares on low-demand routes by an additional 18–22% during Jan–Mar. Bundled offers (seat + baggage) at a perceived discount can stimulate volume without degrading the base fare structure for peak pricing.

Airline Revenue & Pricing Analytics
Tableau Excel Regression
BUSINESS PROBLEM → DATA PREP → ANALYSIS → INSIGHTS → RECOMMENDATION
BUSINESS PROBLEM

An airline company needs to understand what factors most influence ticket pricing across routes and seasons to optimize revenue per seat. Key question: When should fares be raised or discounted to maximize yield?

DATASET INFORMATION
12,000+ROWS
14COLUMNS
KaggleSOURCE
▸ Route, departure city, destination, airline name
▸ Ticket price, class, seat availability, booking window
▸ Travel date, season, duration, stops
DATA PREPARATION
▸ Removed 340 duplicate rows and 12 rows with null ticket prices
▸ Converted date columns from string to datetime format
▸ Engineered "booking_window_bucket" feature (0–7, 8–14, 15–30, 30+ days)
▸ Created "season" column from departure month (Summer/Winter/Monsoon/Off-peak)
▸ Normalized price column — removed outliers beyond 3 standard deviations
ANALYSIS METHODS
Linear Regression Correlation Matrix Descriptive Stats Aggregation Segmentation Trend Analysis
▸ Pearson correlation between booking window and ticket price
▸ Revenue aggregation by route, class, and season
▸ Multi-variable regression: price ~ class + season + booking_window + stops
KEY INSIGHTS
INSIGHT 01
Business class tickets yield 2.4× higher revenue per seat than economy, but account for only 18% of total bookings — representing untapped upsell potential.
INSIGHT 02
Tickets booked 14–21 days in advance are priced 23% lower than last-minute fares, with 91% seat availability — the optimal booking window for budget travelers.
INSIGHT 03
Peak season routes carry a 38% price premium over off-peak. Top 3 routes account for 41% of total annual revenue despite representing only 12% of all routes.
◈ BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION
The airline should implement dynamic pricing tiers that raise fares by 25–35% for bookings made within 7 days of travel on high-demand routes. Introduce a targeted upsell campaign for economy passengers on business-heavy routes (Delhi–Mumbai, Delhi–Bangalore) where business class demand is highest. Off-peak route pricing should be reduced by 15% to stimulate demand and improve load factors during monsoon months.

BUSINESS PROBLEM

Flipkart needs to understand how pricing, discounts, and ratings interact to drive category-level revenue. Key question: Which price bands and discount strategies generate the highest conversion and revenue?

DATASET INFORMATION
18,000+ROWS
16COLUMNS
KaggleSOURCE
▸ Product name, category, brand, MRP, selling price
▸ Discount %, customer rating, number of reviews
▸ Stock status, seller name, product description
DATA PREPARATION
▸ Dropped 820 rows with missing selling price or MRP values
▸ Calculated discount_pct = (MRP - selling_price) / MRP × 100
▸ Created price_band feature: Budget (<₹500), Mid (₹500–₹2000), Premium (>₹2000)
▸ Standardized category names — merged 47 sub-categories into 8 main categories
▸ Converted rating column from string "4.2 out of 5" to float 4.2
ANALYSIS METHODS
Correlation Analysis Group Aggregation Price Segmentation Brand Premium Index Review Density
▸ Correlation: discount_pct vs. rating (Pearson r = -0.31)
▸ Revenue proxy = selling_price × estimated_sales (reviews × 10)
▸ Brand premium index: avg_price / category_avg_price × 100
KEY INSIGHTS
INSIGHT 01
Electronics category drives 52% of total platform revenue while representing only 31% of product listings — indicating higher average order values and stronger purchase intent.
INSIGHT 02
Discounts above 30% correlate with 3× higher review volume (proxy for conversions), but products with >50% discount have 0.4 lower average ratings — suggesting quality perception erosion.
INSIGHT 03
The ₹500–₹2,000 mid-range price band has the highest review density (4.3 avg rating, 287 avg reviews) — indicating the sweet spot for consumer satisfaction and volume.
◈ BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION
Flipkart should cap promotional discounts at 35–40% maximum to avoid perceived quality degradation. Electronics sellers should be incentivized to expand mid-range (₹1,000–₹2,000) SKUs where conversion rates are highest. A category-specific pricing floor strategy would protect brand equity while maintaining competitive positioning against Amazon India.

AI Tools Impact on Workforce Productivity
Python Statistics Power BI
BUSINESS PROBLEM → DATA PREP → ANALYSIS → INSIGHTS → RECOMMENDATION
BUSINESS PROBLEM

Organizations investing in AI tools need to quantify actual productivity gains by department and role type. Key question: Which business functions derive the highest ROI from AI adoption, and what adoption rate is needed to see measurable impact?

DATASET INFORMATION
8,000+ROWS
11COLUMNS
KaggleSOURCE
▸ Employee ID, department, role, AI tool used
▸ Hours saved/week, task completion rate, satisfaction score
▸ Adoption date, usage frequency, training hours invested
DATA PREPARATION
▸ Imputed 156 missing "hours_saved" values using department-level median
▸ Encoded categorical "adoption_level" (Low/Medium/High) as ordinal 1/2/3
▸ Computed productivity_multiplier = post_AI_output / pre_AI_output
▸ Removed outliers: productivity_multiplier > 5.0 (data entry errors)
▸ Grouped departments into 4 functions: Marketing, Operations, HR, Finance
ANALYSIS METHODS
Descriptive Stats ANOVA Test Segmentation ROI Modeling Adoption Curve
▸ One-way ANOVA: productivity difference across departments (p < 0.05)
▸ ROI = (hours_saved × avg_hourly_rate) / training_investment
▸ Adoption curve: adoption_rate vs. productivity_multiplier scatter
KEY INSIGHTS
INSIGHT 01
Marketing departments show 74% AI adoption rate with a 3.2× productivity multiplier — the highest ROI across all functions, driven primarily by content and campaign automation tools.
INSIGHT 02
Operations roles show only 1.8× productivity multiplier despite significant training investment — suggesting AI tools are not yet well-matched to repetitive physical-process workflows.
INSIGHT 03
Employees with >8 hours of AI training show 2.1× higher task completion rate than those with <2 hours — confirming that training investment directly drives productivity gains, not just tool access.
◈ BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION
Companies should prioritize AI deployment in Marketing and Finance functions first, where structured, repeatable tasks yield the highest multipliers. Operations AI investment should be deferred until process-specific tools mature. All AI rollouts should include a minimum 8-hour training program — the data shows training doubles productivity outcomes regardless of tool quality.

Gym Member Performance & Retention Analytics
Tableau SQL Excel
BUSINESS PROBLEM → DATA PREP → ANALYSIS → INSIGHTS → RECOMMENDATION
BUSINESS PROBLEM

A gym chain is losing members at a 34% annual churn rate. Key question: Which member profiles and workout patterns predict long-term retention, and how can facilities be optimized to reduce churn?

DATASET INFORMATION
6,000+ROWS
13COLUMNS
KaggleSOURCE
▸ Member ID, age, gender, BMI, membership type
▸ Workout type, sessions/week, avg session duration
▸ Check-in time, membership duration, churn status
DATA PREPARATION
▸ Filled 89 null BMI values with age-gender group median
▸ Created age_group buckets: 18–24, 25–34, 35–44, 45+
▸ Engineered check_in_hour → time_slot: Morning/Afternoon/Evening/Night
▸ Calculated sessions_consistency_score = sessions_attended / sessions_scheduled
▸ Flagged churned members (no check-in for 60+ days) as churn = 1
ANALYSIS METHODS
Retention Analysis Cohort Analysis Segmentation Peak Hour Modeling Churn Prediction
▸ 90-day retention rate by workout type and sessions/week
▸ Peak hour analysis: check-in counts by hour and day of week
▸ Logistic regression: churn ~ frequency + workout_type + BMI + age
KEY INSIGHTS
INSIGHT 01
Members attending 3–4 sessions per week show 68% higher 6-month retention compared to daily users, who burn out 40% faster — suggesting over-training contributes to dropout.
INSIGHT 02
Evening sessions (6–9 PM) account for 48% of all check-ins but only 32% of equipment capacity — indicating peak-hour bottlenecks that reduce satisfaction and drive churn.
INSIGHT 03
Strength training members have the highest 90-day retention (81%) versus cardio-only members (52%) — goal-oriented training with measurable progression correlates strongly with membership persistence.
◈ BUSINESS RECOMMENDATION
The gym should introduce a "Smart Scheduling" feature that recommends off-peak slots to members and offers 15% fee discounts for morning bookings to redistribute load. A 3–4 sessions/week wellness plan should be marketed as the optimal commitment level, backed by the retention data. Strength training programs should be expanded and prominently promoted, given their strong retention correlation.
